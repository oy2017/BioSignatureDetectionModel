1. Precision at realistic base rates. The grid is balanced by construction, so every
   accuracy in the paper assumes a 50 % prevalence. Recall and specificity are properties
   of the classifier and do not change with prevalence; precision does.

   threshold  recall  specif.    π=0.5    π=0.2    π=0.1   π=0.05   π=0.01
       0.500   97.6%    94.1%    94.3%    80.5%    64.8%    46.5%    14.3%
       0.215   99.3%    90.7%    91.5%    72.9%    54.4%    36.1%     9.8%
       0.900   90.9%    98.0%    97.9%    92.0%    83.7%    70.8%    31.8%
       0.990   72.3%    99.5%    99.3%    97.1%    93.8%    87.7%    57.8%

   at π = 0.10, 50 % precision needs threshold 0.121 and retains 99.7 % recall
   at π = 0.05, 50 % precision needs threshold 0.611 and retains 96.7 % recall
   at π = 0.01, 50 % precision needs threshold 0.986 and retains 76.4 % recall

2. Why per-spectrum normalization helps: the absolute-depth nuisance direction.
   Raw vs normalized features trained and tested on radius-restricted populations,
   training-set size held fixed at 4,000 planets per band.

    radius (R_E)      raw   normalized      gap   depth span
            1-26   80.83%       95.33%  +14.49      4.49 dex
             1-6   74.57%       95.10%  +20.53      4.24 dex
            6-12   84.74%       95.12%  +10.37      2.89 dex
           12-20   88.02%       95.55%   +7.53      2.33 dex

   The normalization advantage falls from +14.5 points on the full grid to
   +7.5 on the narrowest band, tracking the shrinking spread of absolute transit depth.
